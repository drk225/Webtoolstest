What this tool is: a workshop aid for drafting and cleaning up a Bill of Materials — a concept companion to Rev: Ithaca Startup Works' Bill of Materials / Engineering BOM workshops, not a replacement for the official Rev BOM Template.xlsx your team may already be using for production tracking.

Where the checks come from: the "flag" rules below (missing supplier, priced-but-fabricated parts, early-sourcing vendor reminders, etc.) are drawn directly from the guidance given in those workshop decks — they are nudges to double-check your data, not a hard gate. You can save or export a BOM with flags still showing.

Supplier auto-fill: pasting a product link into "Link" fills in the Supplier field by matching the link's own domain (e.g. mcmaster.com → McMaster-Carr) against a short built-in list — nothing is fetched from that link or any other site. For McMaster and Digi-Key links specifically, the "Mfr/Supplier Part No." field is also filled in by reading the part number out of the link's own URL text (this is separate from this tool's own "Part No." column, which is for your own renumbering scheme). If a recognized link also carries a part number and Type of Part hasn't been set yet, Type of Part defaults to "Purchased." If the domain isn't recognized, or a field is already filled in, nothing changes.

Unit Cost auto-fill (prototype, sends data out — read this one): unlike Supplier/Part No. above, a part's price isn't in the link's URL text — it only exists on the rendered page. Pasting a link from a recognized supplier (McMaster, Digi-Key, Mouser, Adafruit, SparkFun, Grainger, Online Metals) into "Link," when Unit Cost is still empty, sends that URL to this tool's own backend (a small function hosted alongside this page), which opens the page in a real headless browser, reads its price the same way a person looking at the page would, and sends just that number back — this is the one place in this tool where something you enter leaves your browser. It checks, in order: a structured price tag the page itself publishes (schema.org pricing data, when present — the most reliable source), then the first dollar amount near anything labeled "price," then the first dollar amount anywhere on the page. A "Looking up price…" placeholder shows on Unit Cost for a few seconds while this runs — it isn't blocking, you can keep working. That heuristic can occasionally grab a list price, a bulk-quantity price, or an unrelated dollar amount instead of the one you want, so double-check Unit Cost once it fills in; if it's wrong, missing, or the lookup fails (unsupported domain, timeout, or the supplier's site blocking the request), just type over it or fill it in by hand — this never blocks or errors the row. Unit Cost also auto-strips "$"/commas/"USD" from whatever lands in it, typed or looked up, so "$8.42" becomes "8.42."

CSV import: "Upload CSV" adds rows from a CSV file into your current BOM by matching each column's header name (e.g. "Part No.", "Link" or "URL", "Qty" or "Quantity") against a short list of accepted names per field, regardless of column order — it doesn't need to exactly match this tool's own export headers. A column whose header isn't recognized is skipped and listed after import. Imported rows are added alongside what's already on your worksheet, not swapped in for it. Use this to bring in a BOM you already have from somewhere else — not needed for a file this tool itself saved; just use Open for that.

Save/Edit per row: click "Save" on a row once its fields are filled in — this locks the row against accidental edits and refreshes the BOM's totals, flags, and cost-driver ranking to include it. Click "Edit" to unlock a saved row and change it again.

Your data: nothing you enter here is uploaded or sent anywhere, with one exception: pasting a recognized supplier link sends that URL (and only that URL) to this tool's own price-lookup backend, described just above — everything else lives in this browser tab only. As a safety net against an accidental refresh or back-navigation, your in-progress work is also autosaved to this browser's local storage (still never leaves your machine) and offered back to you the next time you open this page. Your working file is a real .xlsx spreadsheet — use "Save" or "Save As…" to keep a copy anywhere on your computer (or a synced Drive/Dropbox folder, to share with teammates), and "Open" to pick back up later. It opens normally in Excel or Google Sheets too.
