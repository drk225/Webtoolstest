Break-Even Analysis Worksheet

Separate your recurring per-unit costs from one-time tooling and certification costs, then get the exact number of units you need to sell to hit $0 balance — no more nudging a "units sold" number up and down until the balance looks close enough.

More info
What this tool is: a workshop aid for modeling break-even volume on a hardware product — a concept companion to Rev: Ithaca Startup Works' costing workshops, not a substitute for a supplier-quoted, accountant-reviewed cost model before you commit capital.

1Component / Parts COGS (Per Unit)

Check "Has NRE Cost?" for a part with a one-time tooling or certification fee — that cost moves to the Non-Recurring Costs table below instead of inflating your per-unit cost. Check "Price Breaks" for a part with quantity-based supplier pricing to record up to three quantity/price tiers below that row for reference; "Save" (or "Hide Price Breaks") collapses the tiers without deleting them, and unchecking "Price Breaks" clears them.
More info
Price breaks: check "Price Breaks" on a Parts COGS row if your supplier quotes lower unit costs at higher quantities. Three tier rows appear below that part for you to fill in Qty/Unit Cost reference points; "Save" or "Hide Price Breaks" collapses them without losing the data, and unchecking "Price Breaks" clears them. Tiers don't automatically change the Unit Cost or break-even math — if the solved break-even quantity qualifies a part for a different tier than its current Unit Cost, a "Resolve at break-even volume" prompt appears above so you can apply the correct tier price with one click.

BOM upload: "Upload BOM (.xlsx)" reads a Bill of Materials saved from the BOM Worksheet tool and fills in part names, quantities, and unit costs here, matched by column header name — a BOM you built from scratch in Excel with recognizable column headers (Name, Qty, Unit Cost) works too, not just a file that tool itself saved.

Recurring vs. non-recurring: per-unit costs (parts, assembly, packaging, shipping) recur every time you build a unit; tooling and certification costs (FCC, UL, CE, etc.) are one-time. Mislabeling a certification fee as "tooling" on a part row understates your real per-unit margin — use the "Has NRE Cost?" checkbox on the Parts table and the NRE table below it to keep them separate.

4Break-Even Calculator

More info
Break-even math: Exact Break-Even Units = Total Non-Recurring Costs ÷ (Price per Unit − COGS per Unit). "Your Total Spend" is the full amount you'll spend on COGS and NRE to produce and sell that many units. If price doesn't cover COGS per unit, there is no break-even volume — a warning is shown instead of a number.
Every field below updates live as you edit the tables above — the exact break-even quantity is solved directly, not by trial and error.

5Export

Your data: nothing you enter here is uploaded or sent anywhere. Everything lives in this browser tab only. As a safety net against an accidental refresh or back-navigation, your in-progress work is also autosaved to this browser's local storage (still never leaves your machine) and offered back to you the next time you open this page. Your working file is a real .xlsx spreadsheet — use "Save" or "Save As…" to keep a copy anywhere on your computer (or a synced Drive/Dropbox folder, to share with teammates), and "Open" to pick back up later. It opens normally in Excel or Google Sheets too.
