Formatting your spreadsheet for import
Export your spreadsheet as a .csv file first — in Excel or Google Sheets, use File → Save As / Download → Comma Separated Values (.csv). This tool reads plain CSV only; it can't open a native .xlsx or Google Sheets file directly.

Put one interview per row, with a header row using these column names (any order is fine, and extra columns you don't need are simply ignored):
Column headerRequired?Notes
Customer NameRequiredAlso recognized as "Name" or "Interviewee".
CompanyOptional—
DateOptionalAny text is fine — it's stored as-is, not parsed as a calendar date.
SegmentOptionalThe customer segment this interviewee represents.
Key TakeawaysOptionalAlso recognized as "Takeaways" or "Notes".
Imported interviews start unlinked to every canvas note — a spreadsheet has no way of telling this tool which BMC assumption each interview actually supports, so that part still has to happen here: open each imported interview and check off the notes it validates under "Linked BMC Notes."

What this tool is: an unofficial concept mockup styled for the Dairy Runway Program — a workshop aid for drafting a Business Model Canvas and tracking the customer-interview evidence behind it, in the same customer-discovery spirit as the NSF I-Corps model the program is based on. This page is not an official Cornell, CREA, or Dairy Runway Program deliverable, and isn't affiliated with, reviewed by, or endorsed by them — it's a visual re-skin of a generic workshop tool, built to show what a branded version could look like. The canvas layout is adapted from the Business Model Canvas by Strategyzer AG / Business Model Generation, used under CC BY-SA 3.0.

How linking works: each canvas note can hold a running list of customer interviews that support it. You add that link from the Interview Log — check off which notes an interview validates — and the badge on the canvas note updates automatically. There's no way to hand-type a count; it's always the real number of linked interviews.

Your data: nothing you enter here is uploaded or sent anywhere, including a spreadsheet you import — it's read directly in your browser tab and never leaves your machine. As a safety net against an accidental refresh or back-navigation, your in-progress work is also autosaved to this browser's local storage (still never leaves your machine) and offered back to you the next time you open this page. Your working file is a real .docx Word document — use "Save" or "Save As…" to keep a copy anywhere on your computer, and "Open" to pick back up later. That one file holds your whole canvas, your whole interview log, and all the links between them.
